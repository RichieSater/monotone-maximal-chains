\begin{abstract}
We answer negatively a question of Monakhov and Sokhor asking whether every
finite group admits an unrefinable subgroup chain whose successive indices
are nondecreasing. For every odd prime $p$, we construct a soluble subgroup
$G_p\leq\operatorname{GL}_5(p)$ of order $2^6p^8$ for which no such chain
exists. The construction adapts an example of Kohler and yields an explicit
infinite family; its smallest member, $G_3$, has order $419904$. We determine
the maximal-subgroup index spectra needed for the obstruction and the
relevant conjugacy classes of maximal subgroups. In $G_p$, an increasing
chain, read downward, is forced to begin with two steps of index $p^2$.
After these steps the resulting subgroup has maximal-subgroup indices only
$2$ and $p^4$. Since the downward indices must be nonincreasing, the
$p^4$ option is excluded and all remaining steps would have index $2$.
This would force a subgroup whose order is divisible by $p$ to be a
$2$-group, a contradiction. The argument proves the result uniformly for
all odd primes and does not depend on computation. We also give an explicit
matrix realization for $p=3$ together with independent GAP verification of
the relevant orders, maximal inclusions, conjugacy classes, and absence of
an increasing chain.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

In a finite group $G$, an unrefinable subgroup chain
\[
  1=G_0<G_1<\cdots<G_n=G
\]
has $G_{i-1}$ maximal in $G_i$ for every $i$. We call it \emph{increasing}
if its successive indices $j_i=\lvert G_i:G_{i-1}\rvert$ satisfy
$j_1\leq\cdots\leq j_n$. Monakhov and Sokhor call this a
$<$-chain~\cite{MS2025}. They show that a group with two maximal chains of
the same length, one with nondecreasing indices and the other with
nonincreasing indices, is supersoluble.

Monakhov and Sokhor asked the author whether every finite group has an
increasing unrefinable chain. Finite nilpotent groups do: choose maximal
chains in their Sylow subgroups and adjoin the Sylow factors in increasing
order of the primes. Each step within a Sylow $q$-subgroup has index $q$.
Solubility alone, however, does not suffice.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every odd prime $p$, there is a soluble group
$G_p\leq\operatorname{GL}_5(p)$ of order $2^6p^8$ with no increasing
unrefinable subgroup chain.
\end{theorem}

The construction uses the $t=2$ case of Kohler's example
\cite[Section~4, Theorem~4.1]{Kohler1964}, which shows that bounds on
maximal-subgroup indices need not pass to subgroups. We give a matrix
realization and use its nested maximal subgroups to rule out increasing
chains. The smallest member of the family is $G_3$, of order $419904$.

The reason for the obstruction is that a four-dimensional irreducible
commutator layer lies in the Frattini subgroup of the normal $p$-subgroup.
It therefore contributes no maximal index at the top. Removing either of
two two-dimensional layers makes the commutator layer complemented, but its
index $p^4$ is then too large to follow a downward step of index $p^2$.
The following proposition makes this obstruction precise.

\section{The index obstruction}

Write $\I(A)=\{\lvert A:B\rvert:B\text{ is maximal in }A\}$ for the
maximal-index spectrum of a finite group $A$. Spectra alone do not describe
which maximal steps can follow one another; the conjugacy conditions below
supply this information.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:trap}
Let $p$ be an odd prime and let the finite group $G$ have maximal subgroups
$M_1,M_2$ with a common maximal subgroup $N$. Suppose that
$p\mid\lvert N\rvert$ and
\[
 \I(G)=\{2,p^2\},\qquad
 \I(M_1)=\I(M_2)=\{2,p^2,p^4\},\qquad
 \I(N)=\{2,p^4\}.
\]
Suppose also that every maximal subgroup of $G$ of index $p^2$ is conjugate
to $M_1$ or $M_2$, and every maximal subgroup of $M_i$ of index $p^2$ is
$M_i$-conjugate to $N$, for $i=1,2$. Then $G$ has no increasing
unrefinable subgroup chain.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Read a putative increasing chain downward, so that its indices must be
nonincreasing. Once an index $2$ occurs, all subsequent downward indices
must equal $2$, and the subgroup from which that step starts must be a
$2$-group.

Since $p$ divides the orders of $G$ and both $M_i$, the first two indices
must therefore be $p^2$. By the conjugacy assumptions, we may conjugate
first in $G$ and then in the chosen $M_i$ to make these steps
\[
 G\xrightarrow{\ p^2\ }M_i\xrightarrow{\ p^2\ }N.
\]
At $N$, the bound $p^2$ excludes the index $p^4$, leaving only $2$.
This would make $N$ a $2$-group, contrary to $p\mid\lvert N\rvert$.
\end{proof}

\section{The construction}

Fix an odd prime $p$, put $F=\F_p$, and let
\[
 s=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 t=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 D=\langle s,t\rangle\leq\operatorname{GL}_2(F).
\]
Thus $D$ is dihedral of order $8$. With block sizes $2,1,2$, set
\[
 L=\left\{
 \ell(x,y,z)=\begin{pmatrix}I_2&x&z\\0&1&y\\0&0&I_2\end{pmatrix}
 :x\in F^{2\times1},\ y\in F^{1\times2},\ z\in F^{2\times2}
 \right\}.
\]
The subgroup
$H=\{\operatorname{diag}(A,1,B):A,B\in D\}\cong D\times D$
normalizes $L$, since conjugation $\ell\mapsto h\ell h^{-1}$ sends
\begin{equation}\label{eq:action}
 (x,y,z)\longmapsto(Ax,yB^{-1},AzB^{-1}).
\end{equation}
Define $G_p=L\rtimes H\leq\operatorname{GL}_5(p)$.
It is soluble, being an extension of a $p$-group by a $2$-group, and
$\lvert G_p\rvert=p^8\cdot 8^2=2^6p^8$.

To identify the commutator subgroup and its module structure, put
\[
 X=\{\ell(x,0,0)\},\qquad Y=\{\ell(0,y,0)\},\qquad
 Z=\{\ell(0,0,z)\}.
\]
Multiplication and commutation are given by
\begin{align*}
 \ell(x,y,z)\ell(x',y',z')
   &=\ell(x+x',y+y',z+z'+xy'),\\
 [\ell(x,y,z),\ell(x',y',z')]
   &=\ell(0,0,xy'-x'y),
\end{align*}
where $[a,b]=a^{-1}b^{-1}ab$. In particular, $Z$ is central in $L$, and
$L'=Z$ because the outer products $xy$ span $F^{2\times2}$.
Also $\ell(x,y,z)^p=\ell(0,0,pz+\binom p2xy)=1$, since $p$ is odd.
The Frattini formula gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:frattini}
 \Phi(L)=L^pL'=Z.
\end{equation}

As an $H$-module, $L/Z=X\oplus Y$, identifying each subgroup with its image.
Both summands are irreducible of dimension $2$: $t$ fixes only the coordinate
lines, and $s$ interchanges them. Their distinct action kernels
$1\times D$ and $D\times1$ show that they are nonisomorphic.

The action on $Z$ in \eqref{eq:action} is irreducible of dimension $4$.
Indeed, $D$ spans the full matrix algebra $M_2(F)$: $(I_2+t)/2$ and
$(I_2-t)/2$ are the diagonal matrix units, and multiplication by $s$ gives
the other two. An $H$-invariant subspace of $Z\cong M_2(F)$ is therefore
stable under all left and right matrix multiplications. Any nonzero matrix
in such a subspace yields every matrix unit, so the subspace is all of $Z$.
Thus the required irreducible dimensions are $2,2,4$.

To compute the maximal indices, we use a standard consequence of
Schur--Zassenhaus. If $V$ is an elementary abelian $p$-group and $H$ is a
$p'$-group, the maximal subgroups of $V\rtimes H$ are $V\rtimes J$, with
$J$ maximal in $H$, and the $V$-conjugates of $W\rtimes H$, with $W$ a
maximal $H$-submodule of $V$. For completeness, a maximal subgroup $T$ not
containing $V$ satisfies $VT=V\rtimes H$. Then $W=V\cap T$ is normal,
and $T/W$ is a complement to $V/W$, $V/W$-conjugate to $WH/W$ by
Schur--Zassenhaus. Maximality is exactly the irreducibility of $V/W$.
In our case the first type has index $2$, while a quotient of dimension $d$
in the second type gives index $p^d$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:spectra}
Set
\[
 M_X=(Z\times X)\rtimes H,\qquad
 M_Y=(Z\times Y)\rtimes H,\qquad N=Z\rtimes H.
\]
The subgroups $M_X,M_Y$ are maximal in $G_p$ of index $p^2$, and $N$ is
maximal in each of them of index $p^2$. Their spectra are
\[
 \I(G_p)=\{2,p^2\},\qquad
 \I(M_X)=\I(M_Y)=\{2,p^2,p^4\},\qquad
 \I(N)=\{2,p^4\}.
\]
Every maximal subgroup of $G_p$ of index $p^2$ is conjugate to $M_X$ or
$M_Y$. For each $M\in\{M_X,M_Y\}$, every maximal subgroup of $M$ of
index $p^2$ is $M$-conjugate to $N$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
First, every maximal subgroup $M$ of $G_p$ contains $Z$. Otherwise
$G_p=ZM$, whence
\[
 L=Z(L\cap M)=\Phi(L)(L\cap M).
\]
The defining property of the Frattini subgroup then gives $L\cap M=L$,
contradicting $Z\nleq M$.

It follows that the maximal subgroups of $G_p$ are the inverse images of
those of $G_p/Z=(X\oplus Y)\rtimes H$. Since $X,Y$ are nonisomorphic
irreducible modules, the only maximal $H$-submodules of $X\oplus Y$ are
$X$ and $Y$. The semidirect-product description above gives the spectrum
$\{2,p^2\}$ and the two stated conjugacy types.

In $M_X$, the normal subgroup $Z\times X$ is elementary abelian, with
nonisomorphic irreducible summands of dimensions $4$ and $2$. Its maximal
$H$-submodules are $Z$ and $X$, giving indices $p^2$ and $p^4$;
index $2$ comes from $H$. The index-$p^2$ subgroups are precisely the
$M_X$-conjugates of $Z\rtimes H=N$. The same argument applies to $M_Y$.
Finally, the irreducibility of $Z$ gives $\I(N)=\{2,p^4\}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
The group $G_p$ has the stated order and is soluble. Lemma~\ref{lem:spectra}
and $\lvert N\rvert=2^6p^4$ give all the hypotheses of
Proposition~\ref{prop:trap}.
\end{proof}

\section{The example \texorpdfstring{$p=3$}{p=3}}

The construction gives a compact generating set for $G_3$. Over $\F_3$,
write $e_{ij}=I_5+E_{ij}$, where $E_{ij}$ is a matrix unit. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:explicit-g3}
 \begin{split}
 G_3=\langle &e_{13},e_{23},e_{34},e_{35},
 \operatorname{diag}(s,1,I_2),\operatorname{diag}(t,1,I_2),\\
 &\operatorname{diag}(I_2,1,s),\operatorname{diag}(I_2,1,t)\rangle.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
Indeed, the first four generators give $X$ and $Y$, and their commutators
generate $Z$. The relevant orders and spectra, with the preceding subgroups
specialized to $p=3$, are
\[
\begin{array}{c|r|c}
 A&\lvert A\rvert&\I(A)\\ \hline
 G_3&419904&\{2,9\}\\
 M_X,M_Y&46656&\{2,9,81\}\\
 N&5184&\{2,81\}.
\end{array}
\]

The standalone GAP file \texttt{G3-verification.g} gives the eight generators
as literal matrices, checks the orders, maximal inclusions and relevant
conjugacy classes, and exhaustively excludes increasing chains. The file
and its output are available in the verification archive~\cite{Repository}.
These calculations check the explicit example; the proof for every odd $p$
is independent of computation and classification.

The construction illustrates how a Frattini layer can conceal a large
maximal index until smaller indices have already been forced. For this
purpose, a few maximal-index spectra together with their conjugacy data
suffice; the full subgroup lattice is unnecessary. It remains to determine
the least order of a counterexample. No minimality of $G_3$ beyond this
family is asserted.
